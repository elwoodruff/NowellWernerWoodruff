Refined Questions and Analysis Choices¶

Central question: What affects the size and length of wild fires in California?

Supporting questions: How are cause and year correlated with size and length of fires? How are prescribed burns correlated with the size and length of fires? What are the spatial relationships between size and length of burns (distance from communities, county, etc.)?

Sub-questions: What methods appear to be more effective in containing wildfires? How is the objective of the fire related to fire size and length (can look here at outlier fires that may have burned longer and bigger and what their purpose and cause may have been)?

Variables of interest: Independent: cause of fire, year of fire, prescribed burn; Dependent: size of fire (acres), length of fire (days)

Intended comparison: For our first analysis, we are going to focus on the linear relationships between cause and yea of the fire and the size of the fire, using bar graphs and ANOVA testing to test for correlation between the variables.

Data limitations: We have a lot of data but not for all variables, so for every additional analysis we do, we will have to make decisions about how to address the N/A values.

Analytical Approach¶

To answer our first question of how cause and year are correlated with the size of fire, we are going to look at ANOVA testing to compare the categorical variables of cause and year with the numerical continuous variable of acres burned.

We can use ANOVA to compare these variables because we are looking to compare the linear relationship between a categorical variable (cause), and a numerical value (area burned).

# Perform ANOVA
model = ols('GIS_ACRES ~ C(CAUSE)', data=fire25).fit()
anova_table_cause = sm.stats.anova_lm(model, typ=2)
anova_table_cause

The p-value of the ANOVA test comparing cause and acres burned is small (p<0.05), which indicates that we should reject the null hypothesis and can conclude that cause is correlated with the acres burned in a fire.

Analytical Approach Moving Forward¶

The sum of squares for cause and year is very small in the ANOVA and ANCOVA tests, and therefore we may want to explore log transformation of our area burned data. We also may need to do this for our duration data, which also may have a strong right skew, to have our data approach a normal distribution.

Moving forwards, ANOVA and ANCOVA are the tests we can use, because our independent variables are categorical and our dependent variables are numerical and continuous, for our initial questions.
